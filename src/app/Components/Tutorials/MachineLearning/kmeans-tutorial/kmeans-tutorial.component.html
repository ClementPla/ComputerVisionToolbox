K-Means & EM

The Algorithm

K-Means alternates between two steps until convergence:

E-Step (Expectation)

Assign each point to the nearest centroid based on Euclidean distance.

c(i) = argmin_k ||x(i) - μ_k||²

M-Step (Maximization)

Move each centroid to the mean of its assigned points.

μ_k = (1/|C_k|) Σ x(i)

Convergence

The algorithm converges when assignments no longer change, or centroids move less than a threshold.

WCSS (Inertia)

Within-Cluster Sum of Squares measures how compact clusters are. Lower is better.

WCSS = Σ_k Σ_i∈C_k ||x(i) - μ_k||²

Why EM?

K-Means is a special case of Expectation-Maximization (EM) for Gaussian Mixture Models with equal, spherical covariances.

  • E: Compute cluster responsibilities
  • M: Update cluster parameters

Limitations

  • Sensitive to initialization
  • Must choose K in advance
  • Assumes spherical clusters
  • Can converge to local minima
